Research

Risk and resilience

We use quantum sampling and amplitude estimation to study rare events in supply chains, disaster bonds and inventory planning. Classical optimisation translates those scenarios into decisions such as how much stock to hold and how to reroute shipments.

  • Compound-disruptions whitepaper
  • Quantum estimation for inventory planning
  • Maritime energy security

Constrained quantum optimisation

We study how to encode constraints such as vehicle capacities and knapsack limits in quantum circuits. Our work includes custom penalties that avoid extra slack qubits, circuits that preserve feasibility, and variational imaginary-time evolution as an alternative to QAOA.

  • Custom-penalty QAOA
  • Variational imaginary-time evolution
  • Feasibility-preserving vehicle routing

AI for hybrid solvers

We use learning to tune penalties, reduce problem size and allocate work between quantum and classical solvers. AutoQResearch uses a language model to propose solver policies, then tests them on graph and vehicle-routing problems under a fixed evaluation protocol.

  • Beyond QAOA survey
  • AutoQResearch
  • Learning-based graph shrinking

Quantum reinforcement learning

We study whether quantum circuits that respect problem symmetries can learn routing policies on small instances and transfer them to larger ones. Our experiments examine when that idea works and how simulation cost, sampling and hardware noise limit transfer.

  • Depth-1 equivariant circuits
  • Limits of cross-size transfer

Hardware benchmarking and error mitigation

We benchmark optimisation methods on real quantum processors and track how compilation and noise affect the solutions. We also study ways to reduce the effect of noise, including light-cone cancellation, probabilistic error cancellation and virtual distillation.

  • Hardware benchmarking
  • Light-cone cancellation
  • Virtual distillation bounds

Fault-tolerant resource estimates

We estimate the resources quantum algorithms would need on fault-tolerant systems. Current projects examine the arithmetic cost of derivative pricing and the gate counts of quantum factoring.

  • Quantum derivative pricing
  • Resource estimates for quantum factoring
